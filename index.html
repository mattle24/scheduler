Hard Constraints (always enforced)

  • No team plays twice on the same day
  • No team plays 3+ games in any 4-day window
  • Max 1 weekday game per Monday–Friday span
  • AL/NL: each team plays every intra-league opponent at least once

3Generate schedule

Check your setup, then generate your schedule.

    ▶ help How does the scheduler work?

    1. Build the matchups. First, the scheduler figures out who plays whom. It generates a balanced set of matchups so every team plays the right number of games and faces a good mix of opponents. If a division uses the AL/NL league split, it makes sure teams play everyone in their own league before scheduling cross-league games.

    2. Assign home and away. Each matchup gets a home and away team, balanced so no team is stuck at home (or away) too often — both overall and against each individual opponent.

    3. Place games on the calendar. The scheduler tries 250 different arrangements, placing games into available field times. Weekend games go first (since weekend availability is most limited), then weekday games fill in around them. It respects hard rules — no team plays twice in one day, no team plays 3 games in 4 days, and at most one weekday game per week. Among legal options, it picks available field times that spread games out evenly across times and fields.

    4. Fine-tune with simulated annealing. The best arrangement from step 3 gets polished. The scheduler makes thousands of small tweaks — swapping game times, moving games to different available field times — and keeps changes that improve the schedule. When other divisions are already scheduled, these tweaks also count the penalties for sharing a field with another division, so polishing one division doesn't undo the field grouping between divisions. This is like shaking a jigsaw puzzle gently to settle pieces into better positions.

    5. Consolidate fields. If a field only has 1–2 games on a given day and another field that day has more, the scheduler tries moving those games over so they pack consecutively — reducing the number of fields that need to be staffed.

    6. Repack game windows. For each field+day with multiple games, the scheduler tries every possible consecutive block of available field times and picks the tightest, best-scoring arrangement. It repeats until nothing improves.

    7. Rebalance home/away. After all field time changes are final, the scheduler does one last pass to fix any home/away imbalances that field consolidation may have introduced.

    8. Score and compare. Every schedule is scored on things like: Are games spread evenly across weekends? Do teams get a fair mix of available field times and fields? Are there awkward back-to-back situations? The Sat/Sun Balance card shows the Saturday/Sunday percentage split for the team furthest from 50/50. You can adjust how much each factor matters using the scheduling priorities above.

    9. Coordinate across divisions. When scheduling multiple age groups, divisions are scheduled in priority order (top to bottom in the table). After the first pass, the scheduler goes back and re-optimizes each division, trying to group games from the same division together on each field — which makes life easier for umpires and families.

    10. Review and edit. In Results, check each division’s rest and day-of-week balance, scan opponents and tight stretches on the timeline, and review how often each pair of teams meets. Review thresholds control the highlights. They leave scheduling priorities and hard rules in effect. Open a game from the timeline to preview a move or swap in Edit, including across divisions. Slot edits change when and where teams play. Pairings are set during generation. Applying or undoing a change updates Results and the CSV immediately. The latest schedule restores after refresh when browser storage is available. Undo history lasts for the current session. Setup changes take effect when you generate again.
